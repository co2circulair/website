Markets

One source of green CO2 captured from air.
Three ways to use it, and one to store it.

SMART-DAC captures CO2 from ambient air at around 420 ppm and produces 99.5% pure CO2 at the place where it is needed, wherever sustainable energy is available.

What happens next depends on who uses it. We see four routes: use the CO2 directly, turn it into an energy carrier, use it as a building block for a material that lasts, or store it, although that is not the preferred use of such a valuable product.

  • Route 1Direct useGreenhouses, beverages, food processing, manufacturing, water treatment
  • Route 2Energy carrierE-methanol, e-methane, e-SAF
  • Route 3Long-lasting materialsPlastics, building materials, carbon-based products
  • Route 4StorageUnderground storage (CCS)
Route 1, direct use

Greenhouses: produce green CO2
where and when the crops need it.

Growers add CO2 to their crops during daylight hours. In the Netherlands it can arrive through the OCAP pipeline, which carries CO2 captured from industry.

SMART-DAC makes the CO2 at the greenhouse itself, captured from the air, with an installation that can be sized to demand and run on demand. No pipeline, no truck, no storage, no heat: only electricity.

The same logic for other markets

The same logic applies to other markets that already buy and use CO2 today, such as beverages, food processing, manufacturing processes and water treatment. A SMART-DAC installation produces the CO2 on demand, at the location where it is needed, matched to consumption.

Route 2, energy carrier

E-methanol: a fuel made with CO2
from air, wind and water.

Combined with green hydrogen produced from sustainable energy such as wind power, the CO2 from air becomes e-methanol. For example, one 40-foot container captures around 200 tonnes of CO2 a year, enough for about 175 cubic metres of e-methanol.

Because the installation is modular, the fuel chain can be built on a small scale, next to where the electricity is available, such as a wind turbine or a small harbour, or on a larger scale, such as big harbours, transport hubs, or large wind and solar parks.

The same logic for e-methane and e-SAF

The same logic applies to e-methane. It can be produced at wind and solar parks at times when there is surplus electricity, for example in summer or during the day, and fed into the existing gas grid, to be used when it is needed, for example in winter and at night.

It also applies to e-SAF, sustainable aviation fuel made with CO2 from the air.

Surplus power

Wind and solar parks increasingly produce more than the grid can take. That power can be used on site to capture CO2 and make hydrogen, and stored as methanol or methane. The turbines keep running, and the energy has a buyer.

Route 3, long-lasting materials

Materials: carbon that stays put.

CO2 can be built into plastics, building materials and other carbon-based products, where it stays for decades.

Not only the CO2 itself: the CO2-loaded absorbent from our process can serve as a feedstock for these products too.

Route 4, storage

Storage: carbon capture and storage.

CO2 from SMART-DAC can also be stored underground, in carbon capture and storage.

It is possible, but the high quality of SMART-DAC CO2 makes it a valuable product to use rather than to store.
